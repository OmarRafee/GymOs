# GymOS: Pilot Business & Launch Plan

## Product position

**GymOS helps independent gyms run a smoother member experience from one clear workspace.** Start with the daily jobs that create the most friction: knowing who is active, checking members in, seeing club capacity, and keeping the team aligned.

**First customer:** one established gym in Egypt with one or more branches, an engaged owner, and a team willing to run a short pilot. The best first partner has a known member list and can share a safe sample export plus details about its attendance system.

**Pilot promise:** make front desk operations and member access easier to understand, while leaving the gym's existing membership source of truth in place until the integration is proven.

## What to sell first

Lead with the pilot workflow: staff can find a member, understand membership status, record or reconcile a visit, and see branch occupancy. Add class booking and trainer workflows only after staff confirm they solve a frequent problem. Defer online payments, nutrition, shop inventory, and multi-gym subscriptions until the gym asks for them and the operating model is known.

Do not claim that integrations, live occupancy, secure member QR access, payments, or mobile apps are ready until they are connected and verified. The current browser app is a product prototype with sample data.

## Discovery questions for the gym owner

1. Which system owns member records, contracts, freezes, and renewals today?
2. What scanner or access-control hardware is used, and can the vendor provide approved exports or API documentation?
3. How are missing check-outs, duplicate scans, guests, and staff visits handled?
4. What should happen when a membership is expired, frozen, or under review?
5. Which branches, class types, booking windows, cancellation rules, and capacities matter for the pilot?
6. Which staff roles need access, and what should each role never be able to see or change?
7. What does a successful 30-day pilot look like in measurable terms?

Request synthetic or redacted sample records first. Agree who owns each data field before importing any real member information.

## A practical pilot offer

- **Length:** 4–6 weeks after workflow discovery and safe data setup.
- **Scope:** one organization, agreed branches, staff dashboard, member lookup, attendance and occupancy workflow; classes only if prioritized.
- **Success measures:** time to find a member, check-in correction rate, occupancy reconciliation, staff adoption, class fill rate if enabled, and number of unresolved integration failures.
- **Working rhythm:** named gym owner, one front-desk champion, weekly review, written issue log, and a clear rollback to the gym's existing process.
- **Commercial approach:** define pilot setup/support costs and post-pilot pricing in a written proposal. The blueprint explicitly excludes GymOS SaaS billing from the pilot; do not build subscription billing before validating willingness to pay.

## Landing-page copy starter

**Headline:** A better day at the gym starts with a clearer picture.

**Subhead:** GymOS brings member status, check-ins, classes, and club operations into one simple workspace for your team.

**Primary call to action:** Book a pilot conversation

**Supporting points:**

- Find member and membership details without switching between tools.
- Give the front desk a clear view of attendance and club capacity.
- Add class and trainer workflows when they fit your club.
- Start with one gym and build around the systems you already use.

**Trust note:** Integrations and features are configured with each pilot gym. Do not publish unsupported uptime, security-certification, or integration claims.

## First-month outreach

1. Make a list of 15–20 independent gyms in the target city and identify the owner or operations manager.
2. Ask for a 20-minute workflow conversation; learn how check-in, membership questions, and occupancy are handled today.
3. Demo the prototype with clearly labeled sample data and ask the team to walk through a real busy-hour scenario.
4. Offer a scoped pilot only after confirming the scanner/vendor path, data ownership, and staff champion.
5. Publish short, consented case notes after measurable results; never use member photos, data, or gym branding without written permission.

## Brand and product improvements

- Confirm the product name, trademark availability, logo, and domain before public promotion.
- Replace the sample “Downtown Club” identity and sample figures with the pilot gym's approved branding and representative synthetic records.
- Agree Arabic/English language needs, Egypt timezone, EGP formatting, and a readable mobile-first member flow.
- Validate color contrast, keyboard access, and small-screen layouts with actual front-desk staff.
- Use short product demos and operational before/after stories as the first marketing assets; show real results only after a pilot measures them.
